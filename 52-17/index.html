What is the 52/17 rule?

The 52/17 rule is a deep-work rhythm with one simple contract: work with full focus for 52 minutes, then take a 17-minute break — and during the break, truly unplug. Stand up, walk, look at something far away, make a drink, no screen. The sprints are long enough to actually get somewhere (write the section, fix the bug, finish the chapter), and the breaks are long enough to genuinely recover — not just stretch your wrists at the same desk. This free 52/17 timer runs the whole cycle: press play, watch the sprint energy drain over 52 minutes, and when the break window opens you get 17 minutes with rotating unplug ideas. Six sprints is about 6 hours 54 minutes of focused work — a full, honest day.

Where does 52/17 come from?

From data, not opinion. In 2014 the time-tracking company DeskTime analyzed the behavior of its most productive users — the top 10% by output — and found they naturally worked in 52-minute sprints separated by 17-minute breaks. Top performers treated the sprint like a race: intense, single-tasked, no distractions. Then they fully detached until the next one. That's what makes 52/17 different from most productivity methods: it isn't a rule someone invented on a whiteboard — it's the rhythm the most productive people were already keeping, measured from real work logs.

Is 52/17 better than the Pomodoro Technique?

Different jobs. Pomodoro's 25/5 rhythm is excellent for beating procrastination, starting tasks, and pushing through admin — but 25 minutes is short for deep work (you often reach flow just as it ends), and a 5-minute break barely resets your brain. 52/17 suits the heavy lifting: writing, coding, studying, designing — anything that needs a long runway. And the 17-minute break is a real break: time to walk, eat, or stare out a window. Many people run both: Pomodoro to warm up the day, 52/17 for the deep sessions. If you'd rather set your own pace entirely, try the Flowtime Technique timer.

How to use this 52/17 timer

Press play and give the sprint everything you have — the mascot wilts as your energy drains. When the break window opens, walk away from the screen for 17 minutes. The rotating idea card suggests ways to unplug; extend with +5 if you truly must, but don't make a habit of it. When the break ends, the next sprint starts automatically — the rhythm is the method. Your bars fill as you complete sprints (six is a full day) and the log counts real work time — pausing the timer pauses the log. When you close the tab the session is saved to the history below; opening the tab again starts a fresh log. Pair it with the to-do list below and background sound from the Ambience Studio.
