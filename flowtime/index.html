What is the flowtime technique?

The flowtime technique is Zoë Read-Bivens' creativity-friendly evolution of the Pomodoro method. Instead of slicing your day into rigid 25-minute boxes, you work on one task for as long as you're actually in flow — 15 minutes or two hours — and take breaks sized to how long you worked. There is no countdown bullying you, and no minimum session length: the moment you feel your focus dip, you stop, and your break is already earned. This timer makes the method visible: your duck works while you work, naps while you break, and the break bar charges in real time — so "how much rest have I earned?" is always a glance away.

How are breaks earned?

By default we use the creator's own guideline, applied exactly as she wrote it: stop with under 25 minutes of work and you've earned a 5-minute break; stop between 25–50 minutes and you've earned 8; 50–90 earns 10; beyond 90 earns 15. She calls these suggestions, not rules — the point is that your break is always sized to the work you actually did, never to a fixed clock. Prefer the bar to charge gradually as you work instead of jumping at the brackets? Tick "charge continuously" in Reward Settings (ratio-style interpolation between the same anchors). Prefer one simple ratio instead? Flip the mode (default: 1 break minute per 5 work minutes, Super Productivity's popular choice). End a break early and the leftover rolls into your break bank for next time — stopping on time literally pays.

Flowtime vs Pomodoro — which should I use?

Use Pomodoro (our main timer) when your day is interruptible and you need structure to start. Use flowtime when tasks are unpredictable or deeply creative and a fixed alarm would break your concentration. Many people run Pomodoro for admin and flowtime for deep work — the shared to-do list above follows you between both.
